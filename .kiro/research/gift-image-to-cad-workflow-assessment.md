# MIT GIFT Image-to-CAD Workflow Assessment
**Decision:** Do not treat GIFT as a drop-in runtime room-reconstruction library and do not put it on the six-stage critical path. Approve only a license-gated, optional research experiment for per-object World asset sidecars. The sidecar may propose richer geometry, but Plan, Blockout, approved Canon, `SceneObject` transforms/dimensions, and the camera contract remain authoritative; failure always returns the current deterministic trimesh asset.
GIFT is promising evidence that geometrically verified augmentation can improve a specialist image-to-CadQuery model. It is not evidence that one room image can be reliably decomposed into a coherent, metrically correct room, nor that released GIFT software or weights can currently be embedded here.
## What GIFT is—and is not
The [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27448) defines Geometric Inference Feedback Tuning as **offline training/data augmentation**. A pretrained image-to-CAD model samples many CadQuery programs for training images; a deterministic CAD kernel compares generated solids with ground-truth STEP geometry; GIFT-REJECT retains diverse high-IoU alternatives, while GIFT-FAIL renders executable near misses and pairs those synthetic inputs with original ground-truth code. The augmented examples are then used for ordinary supervised updates. This amortizes expensive search into model weights rather than making search the deployment architecture.
Reported evidence is meaningful but domain-bounded: GenCAD-Code grows from about 163k to 370k examples after roughly one million candidate generations; the full system uses CadQuery/OpenCASCADE and 3B/7B Qwen-VL-family models, with the reported setup using 8×A100 80 GB for experiments. Mean IoU rises from 0.698 to 0.782 (about 12%), the pass@1-to-pass@10 gap falls from 15.5% to 5.2%, and peak baseline performance is matched with about 80% less inference compute. The [MIT News account](https://news.mit.edu/2026/turning-2d-designs-into-3d-models-for-rapid-prototyping-0716) likewise describes a framework that teaches a VLM from successes and corrected failures—not a callable reconstruction SDK.
The important limitation is direct: GIFT needs ground-truth CAD programs/solids for geometric feedback and incurs a substantial one-time offline bootstrapping cost. On 400 real-world OOD images, the paper reports mixed reliability (GIFT valid-sample rate 96.75%, but OpenCASCADE failures 2.45% versus 1.18% for SFT), and absolute OOD IoU is substantially below in-distribution performance. A valid program is not necessarily the correct object, correct dimensions, safe code, useful topology, or a faithful room asset.
## Fit against the six-stage workflow
| Stage | Authority and allowed GIFT role |
|---|---|
| **1. Brief** | Brief/user intent remains authoritative. No CAD generation or feedback into interpretation. |
| **2. Plan** | Floor-plan item IDs, footprints, openings, circulation, dimensions, and placement remain authoritative. GIFT must not add, remove, resize, or relocate objects. |
| **3. Blockout** | Blockout remains the geometric/camera conditioning artifact. GIFT must not infer a replacement shell, layout, or camera. |
| **4. Canon** | The approved Canon remains visual authority. Only isolated crops of an approved revision may be consumed after approval; no full-room reconstruction and no mutation/regeneration of Canon. |
| **5. World** | Sole integration candidate: an optional asset sidecar for one existing `SceneObject`. It may replace that object's visual mesh only after validation while inheriting the object's ID, transform, dimensions, material intent, and physics contract. |
| **6. Compare** | Compare may score or reject the optional mesh against Canon and context. It may not back-propagate geometry into Plan/Blockout/Canon/camera; rejection restores the deterministic asset and records why. |
**Invariant:** this is asset enrichment, not scene reconstruction. Current `WorldSession` provenance and immutable workflow profiles remain intact, and the locked camera contract is never an inference target.
## Recommended optional World asset sidecar
**Flow:** isolated approved-Canon object crop + crop mask/bounds + minimal `SceneObject` context → image-to-CadQuery candidate → hostile-code sandbox → STEP/B-Rep validation → canonical axis/origin normalization → scale/tessellate to dimension-authoritative GLB → per-object sidecar manifest. The caller supplies only one crop and declarative context (`id`, category, description, target bounding box, expected primitive/material cues); it does not supply session files, secrets, full prompts, or writable project paths.
Generated CAD must produce exactly one nonempty solid. Preserve the parametric source and STEP/B-Rep as quarantined provenance artifacts, but publish only a validated GLB. Resolve orientation deterministically, place the origin by a fixed convention, and scale to the `SceneObject.dimensions` envelope. Prefer uniform scale; if source aspect ratios cannot meet each target axis within tolerance, reject rather than silently distort functional geometry. Tessellation tolerances, coordinate conversion, normals, and material assignment must be fixed and versioned.
The sidecar is advisory and atomic: publish only after all checks pass. Syntax errors, forbidden APIs, nonzero exit, timeout/OOM, no/multiple solids, invalid B-Rep, extreme topology, dimension/aspect mismatch, failed STEP or GLB conversion, non-manifold output, NaN/Inf, or provenance gaps produce a typed failure. The object then uses the existing deterministic trimesh `_generate_object_mesh(obj)` result; World assembly continues, Compare labels the fallback, and no partial/generated file is promoted.
Record at minimum: session/workflow profile; object ID and immutable `SceneObject` snapshot/hash; approved Canon revision/hash; crop/mask coordinates and hash; model/provider/checkpoint digest and license decision; prompt template/version, seed and decoding parameters; generated-code hash (and quarantined source); sandbox image digest/policy; CadQuery/OpenCASCADE/converter versions; execution logs/status/resource use; STEP hash and solid checks; orientation/scaling transform; tessellation parameters; GLB hash; quality metrics; disposition (`accepted`, `rejected`, `fallback`) and reason. Never train on user crops or generated artifacts without separate explicit consent and data-governance approval.
## Suitable and unsuitable object classes
**Suitable first:** isolated, rigid, mostly hard-surface, single-solid or simple boolean/revolve/extrude objects with visible silhouette and known bounding box—stools, simple tables, shelving carcasses, cabinets, counters, lampshades/bases, knobs, handles, planters, vases, and uncomplicated fixtures. These align with CadQuery's parametric strengths and can tolerate appearance materials being added later.
**Unsuitable or defer:** room shells, walls/openings/doors/windows whose dimensions come from Plan; articulated or kinematic assemblies; upholstered/soft goods; plants, cloth, cables, food, liquids, hair/fur; ornate carvings and high-frequency decor; transparent/reflective objects with weak silhouettes; severely occluded objects; topology-critical manufactured parts; safety-, load-, tolerance-, or fabrication-critical components; and any crop containing people, sensitive data, third-party IP without approval, or multiple ambiguous objects. Single-view occlusion and perspective make hidden surfaces guesses, so visual plausibility must never be represented as engineering accuracy.
## Strict sandbox threat model
Treat every generated CadQuery/Python program and every resulting STEP/GLB as malicious. Threats include arbitrary Python execution, network or credential access, host file reads/writes, subprocesses, dynamic imports, persistence, fork bombs, CPU/RAM/disk exhaustion, algorithmic-complexity attacks against OpenCASCADE, malformed geometry exploiting converters/importers, oversized meshes, and covert data in logs or metadata. Prompt constraints and AST checks are filters, not isolation.
Execute each candidate in a fresh non-root disposable VM/container boundary with no network, no host/project mounts, no secrets/environment inheritance, read-only root filesystem, small dedicated tmpfs, dropped capabilities, syscall/profile confinement, process/file/CPU/wall-clock/RAM/output-size limits, and an allowlisted fixed wrapper. Reject imports or access to `os`, `sys`, `subprocess`, sockets, filesystem APIs, `eval`/`exec`, dynamic loading, serialization gadgets, FFI, and reflection; permit only the minimum pinned CadQuery surface. Run CAD validation and mesh conversion in separately disposable workers, re-parse outputs, strip metadata, cap faces/edges/vertices, and copy out only hash-verified declared artifacts. Any containment anomaly is a hard experiment stop, not a fallback-quality issue.
## Licensing and release gate
The public [CAD-Coder source repository](https://github.com/anniedoris/CAD-Coder) declares Apache-2.0 for its repository code. That does **not** establish the terms for linked model weights, GenCAD-Code/DeepCAD images or scripts, upstream LLaVA/Qwen components, generated outputs, or commercial distribution. The cited GIFT paper is available under arXiv's publication terms, but the sources reviewed here do not establish a public GIFT implementation, checkpoint, augmented dataset, or deployable model license.
Before any download, training, hosted inference, redistribution, or product use, require an artifact-by-artifact license/SBOM review and written record covering code, weights, datasets, base models, CAD kernel, converters, training rights, output rights, attribution/NOTICE duties, privacy, and commercial-use restrictions. “Open-source CAD-Coder code” must not be shortened to “GIFT is open source.” If a legally deployable checkpoint and reproducible implementation cannot be identified, the experiment is blocked; architecture notes alone do not justify reimplementation against uncertain training data.
## Proposed 24-object, self-owned experiment
Use 24 self-owned or self-created rigid objects (roughly 8 simple extrude/box, 8 revolve/cylindrical, 8 moderate boolean/detail), with documented image and geometry rights. For each, retain measured X/Y/Z dimensions and an independently created reference CAD model or controlled 3D scan, then produce one isolated crop from an approved Canon-like scene plus `SceneObject` context. Freeze the cohort, model/checkpoint, prompt, seed policy, sandbox, converter, and fallback before testing; report every object, including failures, with no cherry-picking. A 20-30 range is acceptable only if all categories remain represented.
Measure: **executable-code rate** (clean sandbox completion); **valid-solid rate** (exactly one valid nonempty B-Rep); normalized and dimension-constrained **solid IoU** against the owned reference, including failures as zero and reporting mean/median/p25; per-axis and maximum **dimension error**; **STEP export/import** and **STEP→GLB conversion** success; GLB **manifoldness/watertightness**, degenerate faces, normals, NaN/Inf and topology/triangle counts; end-to-end and stage **latency** p50/p95 plus peak CPU/RAM/disk; and **safety** counts for policy rejections, timeouts/OOMs, network/host-write attempts, containment anomalies, artifact-limit violations, and successful deterministic fallbacks.
Suggested go/no-go gate for a larger visual pilot: ≥90% executable, ≥80% valid single solids, ≥95% STEP→GLB and ≥95% manifold/watertight among valid solids, median IoU ≥0.70 with p25 ≥0.50, median maximum-axis dimension error ≤5% and p95 ≤10%, p95 latency ≤60 seconds/object on declared target hardware, 100% fallback success, zero unauthorized network/host writes, and zero containment escapes. Also require side-by-side human review to find no systematic category regression. Any safety escape, provenance gap, license ambiguity, or upstream-authority mutation is an automatic no-go regardless of geometry scores.
**Recommendation after experiment:** proceed only as opt-in World enrichment for the suitable allowlist, with a visible fallback/provenance state and per-category quality gates. Do not market it as exact reconstruction, fabrication-ready CAD, or GIFT integration unless the actual licensed GIFT implementation/checkpoint is used and independently reproduced. Keep deterministic trimesh as the default until the pilot clears both quality and safety gates.
