**Decision 31 (John, 2026-09-11, "what am I judging, because there is a whole house") — THE QUESTION IS THE PRODUCT. Designed, researched, not yet built.** John, as the end user: *"its too confusing as a 10 year old end user to understand if i am trying to judge the house or the porch or the house with the porch etc... because i as the end user am unaware of all the behind the scenes work being done."* **THE LAW THIS CREATES, and it outranks the fix: the user is never shown a question that only makes sense if you know how the factory works.** Decision 28 already established the machine half of this (a candidate's label is a computed diff, never a model's sentence). This is the human half. **THE DEFECT, found by John on the Prop Bench:** the factory asks a PART question using a WHOLE picture — lot `hipped-roof-shingle-covering-h1` shows four complete neoclassical houses under the heading "hipped roof", because the prompt pasted an entire house spec into the request for one component (*"…part of a house that is red brick walls, four tall white columns across the front, white painted trim and joinery, hipped shingle roof, neoclassical presidential style, three storeys"*) and an image model reads that as the subject, not as context. A second failure sits beside it: the base component lots carry the OFFICE style block (*"mid-century executive office, dark walnut and aged brass, oxblood leather and deep green palette"*), so a white column renders as a walnut credenza with a column bolted on. **None of the 22 unmeshed lots contains the thing it claims to be, which is why none were ever meshed** — not a stalled queue, nothing worth meshing. **THE RESEARCH (John: "how have other game developers handled that. go research with ollamma").** Two cloud lanes, GPU untouched (the night shift held the 4090): `gpt-oss:120b-cloud` on shipped building/sim games, `qwen3.5:397b-cloud` on real-world configurators. They converged independently on one pattern, and the connector's own unverified-draft rule was applied — per-pattern cost estimates in person-months, a Minecraft `/setblock` picker, No Man's Sky voice commands and a Sims "show only roof" toggle were **discarded as fabricated**; what follows is what survives. **EVERY shipped solution does the same three things: ONE whole object as context, ISOLATED thumbnails of just the varying part as the choices, and the part DIMMED-OR-LIT in the context view while that question is live.** Porsche and BMW Individual keep the car whole in the viewport and show wheel choices as wheel-only renders on neutral grey; IKEA's kitchen planner dims everything except the cabinet doors during the door step. **Two poles worth naming.** *Townscaper* never asks at all — click a spot, the algorithm decides roof-vs-wall from adjacency, and the component vocabulary is invisible because the player never touches it. *Tesla* (and Lennar's "Everything's Included") barely offer choices, and the ones that exist arrive pre-selected and labelled. **The best fix for "which roof?" is often not to ask it.** And the ORDER is construction sequence — footprint → shell → openings → finishes — because roof pitch changes wall height, so asking about siding first means re-asking later; John's architect card already knows a house in that order and the question queue does not use it.

**THE DESIGN John chose (option 1 + his own refinement):** *"the model and software are intelligent enough to bold the ask i.e. what ROOF do you like the most on your house? this is how a house should be built, or they can select from our new home catalogs."* So the question carries the part — the user never reads a separate label:

> ### What **ROOF** do you like best on your house?
> *one big picture of **his** house — roof bright, everything else quietened*
> *four small pictures underneath: just the roofs, nothing else*
> **Not sure?** → *Pick a whole house from the catalog* · **Don't mind?** → *You choose* ✓

**WHY THIS IS CHEAP: the hard piece already exists and is proven.** `twin-layers` (validated 2026-07-15) renders a scene and cuts per-element masks with SAM3. Pointed at a house, ONE render yields both assets — the isolated roof for the thumbnails and the dimmed-context house for the big view — and because all four options are the same render with one part swapped, they are **guaranteed** to be the same house. That guarantee is exactly what the current prompt cannot give and is why four different buildings appeared. No new render line, no prompt surgery on the component path. **The catalog escape hatch also already exists**: the Home Builders Catalog pattern book — 576 plans, 532 plates, `13-home-builders-catalog`, already wired into `architect_card.py`. "Select from our new home catalogs" is a thing John already owns and has never shown the end user. **The "you choose" default is what stops a ten-year-old stalling on a question he does not care about**, and it is labelled, never silent.

**WHAT THIS CHANGES, ranked:** (i) V17's question layer — the ask names the part in the user's words and bolds it, and every part question carries the two escape hatches; (ii) `neighbourhood-service.py` — component candidates are one render with one part swapped, not four independent renders, so "same house" is structural rather than hoped for; (iii) the component prompt — the house-context clause and the office style block both leave the component path (they are what broke all 22 lots); (iv) the question queue follows construction order; (v) the Prop Bench leads with the end-user question and files the factory detail one click away, since John reviews wearing both hats. **Not built. Nothing above has been run.** The 22 broken lots are unaffected by any of it — their true answer is "none of these", which the board still cannot record until the create-only `decision.json` and mandatory-winner defects from decision 30's notes are fixed.
