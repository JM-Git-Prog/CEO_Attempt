**THE 21 "NONE OF THESE", ROOT-CAUSED AND FIXED (2026-09-11, from John's own pass over the Prop Bench).** John reviewed all 43 lots, approved 22, rejected 21 and **corrected 7 winners he had previously mis-clicked** (the floor safe v2→v7, the leather club armchair v1→v3, the wine barrel v1→v2, the walnut desk v2→v3, the steamer trunk v2→v3, the double doors r3 v1→v3, the hipped-roof h1 v4→v3) — which is exactly the correction the board could not record until now, and the reason the eyes proof was held. The rejections are not taste. **Every one of the 21 has a mechanical cause, and the correlation is clean enough to be a test:** of the 14 lots whose prompt carried the executive-office style block, **12 were rejected** — and the only two that survived are an executive office chair and an executive desk, for which that palette is correct. Of the 11 lots whose prompt carried the house-context clause, **8 were rejected** — and the three that survived are the only LARGE features (a hipped roof and two full-width porches); every rejected one is a door, window, column, portico or entrance, i.e. a part too small to judge in a whole-house frame.

**FOUR CAUSES, THREE OF THEM STILL LIVE IN THE CODE UNTIL TODAY.**
1. **Ten house parts took the office palette because they were rendered on 2026-09-04 — the day before `categoryOf()` existed.** That fix (the `building-part` / `prop` taxonomy, `BUILDING-PART-STYLE.txt`, 2026-09-05) was already correct; these lots simply predate it. No code change needed, but **the renders are unusable and the pictures are still on the board**.
2. **`flagpole` was never in `BUILDING_WORDS`, so it still read as a prop today.** `factory-laws.json` has carried **three open flags** on *"tall white flagpole, steel pole, plain"* since 2026-09-05 — denied every take, twice, before John denied it a third time this morning. **FIXED**, along with 17 other words that name a building part and nothing else (stoop, transom, fanlight, newel, cupola, turret, parapet, downspout, weatherboard, quoin, architrave, entablature, rafter, truss, joist, lath, flashing, doorframe, windowsill, frontage).
3. **`trim` was IN `BUILDING_WORDS`, and it is a furniture word.** *"oxblood leather high back executive chair with brass nailhead **trim**"* classified as a building part, so a chair was rendered without the office palette and came back wrong — while its twin, *"high back executive office chair tufted oxblood leather"*, classified as a prop, rendered right, and John approved it. **One word, two lots, opposite verdicts.** The file's own rule says words that also appear in furniture subjects are left out and that "a wrong category is worse than none"; `trim` broke that rule. **FIXED** — removed.
4. **The house-context clause pasted the entire building into a prompt for one part of it.** `buildPrompt` used `houseLook().look` verbatim: *"red brick walls, four tall white columns across the front, white painted trim and joinery, hipped shingle roof, neoclassical presidential style, three storeys"* — a complete instruction for a BUILDING, which an image model reads as the subject. That is why a door prompt returned four whole houses. **FIXED**: a component may now inherit **material and finish only**, from `HOUSES.json`'s own `materials` array (*"finished to match a house of red brick, white painted timber and shingle"*), and a new `MASSING` filter strips storeys, style periods and elevation composition **in code rather than in the data file**, so a house added later cannot reintroduce the fault by putting "three storeys" in its materials list.

**A FIFTH BUG, FOUND WHILE PROVING THE OTHERS: the ledger recorded the wrong style for every building part ever made.** `currentStyle()` was called with no argument at the provenance callsite, so it defaulted to `"prop"` and wrote `art/PROP-STYLE.txt` into the run ledger for assets actually rendered under `BUILDING-PART-STYLE.txt`. The whole purpose of that block, by its own comment, is to make *"which style made this?"* answerable — and for building parts the answer it gave was false. **FIXED** (`currentStyle(category)`).

**THE LAST TWO REJECTIONS ARE HISTORICAL FAULTS ALREADY FIXED, and they are worth recording because both were verified by looking at the pictures rather than inferred.** *Stack of five hardcover books* (2026-09-01): all four takes are books **sitting on a wooden drawer unit with a brass handle** — the exact fault `make-prop.mjs` documents in its own comment ("the word 'drawers' was in the instruction"), fixed on 2026-09-05 by removing the example-parts list; the approved book stack, rendered after that fix, is a clean stack with no drawer unit. *Ceramic jug vase* (2026-07-11): rendered under the retired **1955 American diner** style (chrome and neon, cherry-red and teal) and returned as eight visibly faceted low-poly jugs against an ask that says *"smooth glazed rounded"* — `prop-flags.json` already holds another prop rejected for exactly "the 1955 diner palette before the house style changed".

**VERIFIED, not asserted:** the patched `make-prop.mjs` was exercised through its own exported functions on John's machine — all nine previously-misclassified house parts now return `building-part`, all seven furniture subjects (including both executive chairs) return `prop`, a door prompt contains none of the six massing phrases it used to carry, a desk-fan prompt is byte-unchanged and still carries the office palette, and `node --check` passes. `make-prop.mjs.bak-2026-09-11-noneofthese` is beside it. **NOT DONE and it costs GPU, so it is John's call: the 21 rejected lots have no usable render and need re-rendering under the fixed prompts** — 10 house parts, the flagpole, the oxblood chair, the five-book stack and the vase. **Also still true: the board cannot record "none of these" at all** (a winner is mandatory and `decision.json` is create-only), so all 21 of these verdicts live only in the Prop Bench's own store until that is patched.
