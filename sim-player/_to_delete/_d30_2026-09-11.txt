**Decision 30 (John, 2026-09-11, "Give the sim its own Pick Board (assuming he will get smarter as it loops)") — BUILT; and THE ASSUMPTION IN THE BRACKETS WAS FALSE, so that was built too.** *The bracket first, because it is the more important half.* **Sam was not getting smarter as it looped. He had no memory at all.** Every round started him from the same persona and the same empty head — which is why he asked for a house with a big porch twelve nights running and would have asked for the same couch every night forever. What *was* getting smarter was the APP (the mechanic patches it), the SHELF (the factory builds what he asked for) and the STUDENT (it trains nightly) — never Sam. Nobody said so until now; the sentence in John's brackets is what surfaced it. **`sim-player/remember.py`** is the honest fix: one small JSON head (`sim-runs/sam-head.json`) folded in at the end of every round, carrying only what a ten-year-old would actually carry — the things he asked for and GOT (he stops asking), the things he asked for and NEVER got (he pushes, and **gives up after three nights** — `gave_up_on()` is the most valuable list on this board, because it is the app's failures ranked by a child's persistence), the rooms he has already filled (he starts somewhere new until they go stale after six rounds), and the house he built last time (so tonight's order is not identical). It is **deliberately forgetful**: a memory that remembers everything makes an expert, and an expert is no longer a first-time user, which is the entire value of Sam. What he remembers is shown to him once, on turn one, in his own words. **What the analyzer says he GOT is what counts as having it** — not what he hoped, not what he typed.

**The board itself.** Decision 29 left *judging* at PARTIAL on purpose: Sam could answer a picture wall for his own house, but a **prop** wall (four takes of a couch) writes a line into `CEO-3D-World/art/preferences.jsonl` — **John's taste**, the file the style model learns from — so Sam was never allowed to touch it, and his verdicts died in the round folder. John's card resolves it the clean way. **Every path the Pick Board WRITES is now an env var, each defaulting to exactly what it was** (`pick-server.mjs`: `PICK_STATIONS`, `PICK_PREFS`, `PICK_REROLL`, `PICK_FLAGS`; `PICK_PORT` already existed) — with nothing set, John's board is byte-for-byte the board it always was. **`sim-player/sim_board.py`** runs a second instance on **:8294** whose whole world is `sim-player/sim-board/`: its own stations, its own reroll queue, its own flags, its own `preferences.jsonl`. `PICKBOARD` is now read from the environment by `v17_pick_routes.py`, `v17_neighbourhood_routes.py` and `gap-router.mjs`, and `sam_loop.py` passes it into the sim Living Room and the gap router, so **every wall Sam hangs and every prop job he causes lands in his own bay**. **The loop no longer starts, stops or reads John's board at all** — `:8194` can be up, down or mid-restart and the night is unaffected. **The guard rail is the point, not the port:** `check()` refuses to start if the port is John's, if the folder resolves inside his tools folder, if the prefs file resolves to his taste ledger, or if `pick-server.mjs` on disk has not been patched — a config typo that would have quietly poisoned the training set stops the loop instead of running.

**And Sam got eyes, because otherwise the board is a dice cup.** A prop wall is four PNGs and nothing else; a chooser who cannot see them is not choosing, and a coin flip appended to a preferences file teaches a style model that taste is random — worse than no answer. **`sim-player/eyes.py`**: a small local vision model looks at **one picture at a time** and scores it 1–5 against what was asked for (one at a time, not a grid — every vision model in the garage handles a single image, the scores stay comparable, and a per-picture score is exactly the shape the board already wants). The top score wins; anything scoring 1–2 is **denied** — a real rejection — while a 3 or 4 that merely lost is only "not picked", which is the distinction the board's own code says only John can draw, and the reason the losing half of a preference pair means anything. **If every take scores below 3, nobody wins and all four are denied** — picking the least-wrong one would both put a bad prop in the house and teach the model it was the good one. `LANES["eyes"] = qwen3-vl:8b → qwen2.5vl:7b → minicpm-v:latest → ibm/granite3.3-vision:2b`, all local and small, each proven live by `ask_lane`. **It fails towards silence, always:** no vision model, an unreadable PNG, a reply that is not a number — Sam says nothing, nothing is posted, and the wall stays open for John. A missing opinion costs one night; a fabricated one costs a training set.

**One real bug found by building this,** and it only became visible the first time Sam remembered a round: the sentence a kid types when he is FINISHED (*"I'm proud of it"*) was being filed as a **wish**, which made it a capability-gap row — and, once he had a memory, something he would chase for three more nights. A line that ends the round is not a request for anything; `wish` now excludes it.
